## Journal Record

Senerio 1 
The week after I joined in college

The atmosphere was entirely new and exciting, though overwhelming, on the first day of college. Every day brought something unexpected: managing classes, assignments, hostel life, and new routines. I slowly learned how to handle things on my own: time management, keeping track of expenses, and other minor things, like where to buy daily stuff. It was so different from home, where everything was taken care of without much effort.
One afternoon, something happened that really taught me the value of kindness and quick help. It was a busy weekday, and I had a long to-do list. I had just finished a class and was running around finishing some small yet important tasks before my next session. I needed to buy a few essential items — some stationery, printing papers, and one or two basic things from the campus store. It was one of those days where every minute mattered.

When I finally arrived at the shop, I found myself totally out of cash. Again and again, I looked into my wallet, thinking that I must have left some money somewhere, but there was nothing. The room was far from that area — almost a 15 to 20-minute walk away. Usually, that would not have been a problem, but on that day, I did not have much extra time to spend. A project submission was coming up in the next class, and I had to get all things ready quickly.

I stood there for a few moments, thinking of what to do. Going back to my room would be a waste of precious time, and I might have missed my next class altogether. That was when I saw one of my senior sisters passing by; she had come across as polite and approachable the few times I had met her in the past. So I hesitated for a moment before I asked, but then decided just to explain to her candidly.

I told her that I had run out of cash and needed to buy some important things urgently, but going back to my room for money would be very time-consuming. And without any hesitation, she said right away that she would help me out. She took out some cash, smiled, and handed it over to me, saying, “Don’t worry about it; just finish your work first.” It was such a simple thing, but at that time, it meant so much to me.

I quickly bought what I needed and managed to complete my work on time. Because of her timely help, I didn’t have to run back and forth between the hostel and the store. After getting everything, I immediately sent the amount to her through PhonePe to return the money. She laughed and said, “You could have taken your time, I knew you’d pay me back.” It was such a small act of kindness she did for me, but saved me from unnecessary stress and helped me be on with my tasks.

Later that evening, when I had time to relax, I thought about the whole incident. It then made me realize how important good people are around you, especially in a new place like college. We always tend to underestimate how small actions can go a long way in affecting someone's day. For her, it was just a few minutes and a small amount of money, while it was very helpful for me that day.

That incident also taught me something about the spirit of helping others. In college, when everyone is busy with schedules of their own, someone taking out a moment to help you without expecting anything in return feels genuinely heart-warming. Her kindness inspired me to be the same way towards others. After that day, whenever I saw someone confused or in need — whether it was a junior trying to find a classroom or someone short of change in the canteen — I remembered how my senior had helped me and tried to offer help in my own small way.

This experience also brought about a change in the way I view relations in college. It's easy to think of seniors as strict or distant, but many of them have gone through the same struggles and are willing to help if we approach them politely. That senior sister and I still greet each other whenever we cross paths, and she often jokes about how "cash emergencies" are part of the real college experience. It’s funny now, but at that time, it really was a mini-crisis for me.

Looking back, that day was not at all about borrowing money; it was to learn how support and compassion build relationships. It taught me that being independent doesn't mean doing everything on my own sometimes; it's just about knowing when to ask for help and being grateful for the people who respond. I learned that gratitude doesn't always have to be in the form of some sort of grand gesture. Many times, a simple "thank you" and appreciation mean just as much. Later in the day, I made it a point to thank her properly and to tell her how much I appreciated her quick help. She smiled and said, "That's what seniors are for." Now that I look back, I realize how such small moments shape our college life experiences. They teach us humility, gratitude, and community bonding. The senior who helped me probably doesn't remember the incident now, but for me, it remains one of the most meaningful experiences of my early college days. It reminds me that even the tiniest act of help can make a person's day easier, and a little kindness always goes a long way.


Senerio 2 
date:8th Aug'25
Entering college for the first time, I was equally excited and a bit scared. The whole idea of leaving home, staying in a hostel, and sharing your room with people you barely knew sounded weird. I didn’t know what to expect-would we get along? Would we have the same habits? Would I feel at home? But looking back now, I can say with full heart that I got incredibly lucky. My roommates, Nethra, Mansi, and Amirtha, turned out to be much more than just people I share a room with. They became my family away from home, my support system, and my favorite part of college life.
All three of them made me feel comfortable right from day one. I came into the room with my bags and a nervous smile; they were so warm that my anxiety just melted away. I didn't feel like a stranger but felt as if I had already belonged. Nethra spoke first, lightly joking about how unpacking days are always such a mess, something that just made me laugh. Mansi offered me some snacks-which, really, is the fastest way to anyone's heart-and Amirtha helped arrange things in an orderly manner. That’s how it started — with laughter, kindness, and a simple act of sharing space.

As time passed, we grew to find out how different and yet well-matched we were. Nethra is composed-always organized, always reminding us about deadlines, and somehow managing to stay composed even while everything around us is chaotic. She has this quiet strength that keeps all of us grounded. Whenever I'm stressed or overthinking, Nethra patiently listens and gives the most practical advice. She never judges, never interrupts-just understands. And I really look up to her for being so mature and thoughtful; it's like she has this comforting energy that makes everything feel manageable.

Then there is Mansi, the bundle of joy and chaos altogether. She's the one who keeps our room lively no matter what. Be it her impromptu dance moves, her funny one-liners, or the way she tells totally irrelevant stories, she can surely make anyone smile on even the worst days. She's the reason our room never feels dull. When we're tired after long classes, Mansi always manages to lift our mood with her humor or some crazy idea — like midnight Maggie sessions or impromptu karaoke nights. She may never realize how she spreads happiness wherever she goes.

And Amirtha — she's our creative soul. There's something so refreshing about her personality. She loves to talk about everything-from movies to deep life thoughts-and she brings a sense of excitement even into the most mundane things. She is caring, expressive, and has this amazing way of making us feel seen and valued. Whenever any one of us is feeling low, Amirtha's the first one to notice. She'll sit beside you, listen, and say just what you need to hear. She has this warmth that makes you feel like everything will be okay.

Living together has been such a beautiful, entertaining journey of learning and laughter. There's this rhythm that builds up: chaos with comfort mixed into one balance, comprehensible only by roomies. Our room has become more than just a sleeping place; it has turned into a hub full of shared stories, inside jokes, and memories that will always stay in our hearts.

We help each other through everything: exams, projects, bad days, and homesickness. The moment one of us falls sick, the rest of them instantly become caretakers. When one person’s alarm rings in the morning, somehow all of us wake up, though not always willingly. When we get ready for class, there’s always a mini fashion discussion: borrowing accessories, asking opinions, and sometimes teasing each other’s choices.

The best part of our bond is how natural it feels. We don’t have to try hard to get along. Some nights we sit together, talking about life till late; other nights, we simply sit in silence, doing our own thing but still connected. We celebrate the small things, be it a birthday or good grade, or even just making it through a hard week, with so much enthusiasm that often our room feels like a festival.

There have also been times when we comforted each other through tears, stress, or frustration. College life can be overwhelming, but having them around makes even the worst days bearable. If I ever feel down, Nethra's calmness, Mansi's laughter, and Amirtha's comforting words immediately lift my spirit. We remind each other to take breaks, eat properly, and focus on the positives.

We've also learned a lot of fun traditions together, such as our Sunday "room cleaning playlist," where we clean, dance, and sing all at the same time, or our late-night gossip sessions starting with one small story and just going on for hours. Sometimes we cook together or order food and binge-watch something random, just to unwind. And honestly, these little routines have become the highlights of our week.

What amazes me most is how we all balance each other out. When one of us is too serious, someone else lightens the mood. When someone's lazy, the others motivate them to move. It's a perfect mix of personalities which somehow fit so well. I've realized that the best part of hostel life is not the freedom or independence; it's the friendships that make all that meaningful.

Each of them taught me something different. Nethra taught me the virtues of patience and how to keep calm in a turmoil. Mansi taught me how to be joyful in the present and smile at the little things in life. Amirtha taught me the virtues of empathy and connectedness. Together, they have made me a better person-more understanding and more joyful, and unafraid to take on life's challenges. College will eventually end, and we will all disperse to different places, but the memories created together will last a lifetime. I know that many years down the line, every time I think of college, the first thing to strike my mind will not be the classrooms or grades; it will be Nethra, Mansi, and Amirtha, and the laughter, support, and love we shared. They have shown me what the feeling of true friendship is all about: being there with each other for everything, celebrating every little joy, and turning even the hardest days into one that is worth smiling over. And for that, I am forever grateful.


Senerio 3 
Date:8th Aug'25



The first day of college is deeply engraved in every person's memory. It is filled with excitement, nervousness, and curiosity about what is next. I still remember walking through the campus gates for the very first time-a bag full of essentials, a schedule in my hand, and a head full of questions. The buildings looked huge, the crowd seemed endless, and I had no idea where to go or what to do next.

While struggling to determine my way, a senior came up to me upon noticing my puzzled expression, with a bright smile on his face. He only asked, “Are you new here?” and upon nodding, he said, “Don’t worry, I’ll help you out.” That single moment of kindness turned what could’ve been a confusing day into one I’ll always remember fondly.

The seniors patiently took me around campus, showing me the main areas: classrooms, laboratories, the library, the canteen, and even the notice boards where all important updates are put. They shared small but really helpful tips: where to get printouts quickly, which professors are strict about attendance, and what time it's best to go to the canteen before it starts to get crowded. Their guidance made everything feel a lot less intimidating.

What really impressed me most, though, was their attitude. Busy as they were, they didn't seem to hurry or act as if they were doing me a favor. They talked nicely to me, answered all my questions, and even checked to make sure I understood the directions properly. I felt as if I had known them for years, not just since an hour ago or so. Friendly and encouraging-that's just what I had needed to feel comfortable and welcome on this campus that only a few hours earlier had felt completely new and overwhelming.

Later in the day, when I couldn't find my next classroom, I called that senior again to help me out. Without the slightest problem, they guided me once again and ensured I reached there in time. It might seem insignificant to someone who has been here long enough, but for a new, insecure kid, it meant much more. In that way, their help saved me from unnecessary confusion and gave me the confidence to move on my own.

Over the next few days, that senior continued to be supportive. He would check in whenever we crossed paths and ask me how I was settling in. He also helped me understand how things worked at college-from managing time between classes and assignments to extracurricular activities. He even encouraged me to step out of my comfort zone and attend my first college club meeting.

But what really stood out about this senior was how approachable they were: there was never any sense of hierarchy or seniority; rather, they treated me like a younger friend. They frequently shared their own first-day experience of how someone had once helped them too, saying, “Everyone needs a little guidance when they’re new.” That sentence stuck with me. It showed how kindness can create a chain of good deeds — someone helps you, and one day, you help someone else.

Their support has made the greatest difference in my early college experience. Anytime I was uncertain about any process, event, or just college life in general, I knew I would get straightforward and honest advice from them. They never made me feel like I was asking a silly question. In fact, they encouraged me to ask more, saying that learning starts with curiosity.

As weeks went by, I began feeling more confident and independent, started exploring new places on campus, making friends, and helping other new students whenever possible. Yet, deep down, I always remembered how this smooth transition into college life was possible, and that was because of the patience and kindness of that one senior.

Looking back now, I realize how a small act of help can make such a big difference. That senior probably didn't think much of it — to them, it was just another day helping a junior — but for me, it completely shaped my impression of college life. Instead of fear and confusion, I felt welcomed and supported.

That experience taught me that college is not only classes and exams but about people: people who guide, support, and make your journey meaningful. I told myself when I get to be a senior, I'll do exactly the same thing. I would help newcomers find their way, share tips, and make them feel at ease just the way someone once did to me. Even now, whenever I see that senior around campus, I feel thankful. The little acts of kindness on their part have served to influence me in no small way. They taught me that at times being kind and spending a few minutes to help others can change how they perceive the world completely. Thanks to that experience, my first day of college became not just a beginning of academics but a beautiful reminder of how kindness builds community and how one helpful person can make an entire new environment feel like home.
